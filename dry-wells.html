DRY WELLS

A place for water to go when there’s nowhere else.

A dry well is an underground chamber that collects runoff from downspouts, French drains or surface drains and lets it soak slowly into the soil. It’s the answer when your yard has no swale, street or retention area to send water to.

THE BASICS

How dry wells work in Southwest Florida

We dig a pit, install a dry well chamber or build a gravel reservoir wrapped in filter fabric, and pipe your runoff into it. The well holds a burst of storm water and lets it drain into the surrounding soil over time. In Southwest Florida the water table can rise close to the surface in the rainy season, which limits how much a dry well can absorb. That’s why we check your soil and groundwater before recommending one, and tell you honestly if another outlet will work better.

QUESTIONS

Straight answers about dry wells.

Will a dry well work in my yard?

It depends on your soil and how high the water table gets in summer. We check both during your free estimate. If a dry well isn’t the best fit, we’ll recommend a different outlet.

How big does it need to be?

Size depends on how much roof and yard area drains into it. We size it for your property rather than using one standard unit.

Can it be hidden?

Yes. The dry well sits underground and is covered with sod, mulch or rock. Some include a small inspection cap at the surface.

Do I still need gutters or drains?

A dry well is the endpoint. It works with downspout extensions, French drains or surface drains that bring water to it.
